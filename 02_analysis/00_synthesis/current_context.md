# Current Context Baseline

Date prepared: 2026-06-09

This note captures the initial verified context used to seed the folder structure. It should be updated as source review deepens.

## County Study Scope

The County's Whiteman Airport Land Use Study site identifies the study scope as:

- existing airport conditions
- airport closure process and closure implications
- stakeholder feedback and preferred options
- economic impact analysis for land-use options

Source: SRC-002

## Contract and Study Structure

The October 22, 2024 DEO board report requested authority to amend the Trifiletti Consulting contract from $600,000 to $1,900,000 for the Whiteman Airport economic impact analysis and land use study. The board report describes a four-phase approach:

- confirmation of airport closure process
- existing conditions analysis
- options development incorporating stakeholder input
- economic impact analysis of preferred options

Source: SRC-003

## FAA Constraint Baseline

FAA correspondence dated January 21, 2026 states that Whiteman has accepted multiple AIP grants and that land acquired with AIP funding can remain federally obligated until released by FAA. The letter also frames FAA approval around civil aviation benefit, meaning non-aviation redevelopment value cannot be the sole basis for airport closure or release.

Sources: SRC-005, SRC-006

## Safety Event Baseline

The NTSB preliminary report for accident WPR26LA164 states that on April 20, 2026, a Cessna 172S, registration N2126F, sustained substantial damage near Whiteman Airport and the pilot was seriously injured. The preliminary report places the aircraft on final approach to runway 12 before impact with powerlines and terrain.

Source: SRC-004

## Analysis Implications

The framework should treat Whiteman as a constrained systems-of-systems problem. The County decision space appears to be shaped by at least five interacting domains:

- legal and FAA release constraints
- aviation network and operational continuity
- community health, safety, and environmental justice
- land-use, housing, and economic value
- governance, legitimacy, funding, and implementation risk
